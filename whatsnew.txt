# Every release's notes, newest first, shown by What's new (WhatsNewWindow.cs).
# "## version | date | title", then the notes: "- " a point, a line in ** ** a heading, anything else a paragraph.

## 1.0.66 | 2026-10-30 | A filter stays with its folder
**Fixes**
- A filter no longer follows you to another folder, drive or tab, where it could show an empty list over a full folder. Going somewhere else clears it; refreshing the same folder keeps it.
- While a filter is on, the bottom of the pane says how much it shows: "3 of 110 match “fra”", instead of what the folder holds.

## 1.0.65 | 2026-10-29 | Safer renames, honest transfers, an Undo that's sure
The same user read 1.0.64's code and found what could still go wrong. All of it is fixed.
**Renames and names**
- A batch rename that fails halfway now **puts every name back**, also when names were swapped among themselves. If one can't go back, you're told which, and Ctrl+Z tries again.
- If File Labs stops in the middle of a batch rename, Ctrl+Z can still put the names back.
- **Keep both** no longer gives two files the same new name when they come from different folders, and two files of the same name in one copy are asked about like any other clash, instead of one failing.
**Transfers say what happened**
- A copy or move with files that failed or were skipped says so in its title: "Moved 4,990 of 5,000 · 10 failed", not "Moved 5,000".
- A file that arrived just as you pressed Cancel is counted, and Undo knows about it.
- Pause and Cancel also work while a move between drives is removing the originals.
**Undo you can trust**
- Undo puts things back under their **exact old names**, also ones Keep both had renamed.
- After a delete, Undo brings back **that very item** from the Recycle Bin, never a later one deleted from the same place.
- Something you put in place of an item since is **left alone**, and Undo says so.
- Each transfer writes down what it did as it goes, so Undo survives even a crash. The history is saved in one piece, off to the side first, and a failure to save is said.
**Smoother with big jobs**
- The Recycle Bin window reads, restores and deletes in the background, with the count as it goes. So do batch renames, putting deleted items back, and Ctrl+C inside an archive.
- A folder where thousands of files arrive at once refreshes once, not once per file.
- A search with no limit hands its results to the list a few hundred at a time, as fast as the list takes them.
- The Drop bin changes in place, and the rename preview waits for a pause in typing when thousands of files are selected.

## 1.0.64 | 2026-10-29 | Moves you can stop, and an Undo that remembers
The same user's second mail: what big selections and moves could do, and how to take them back.
**Moves**
- A move on the same drive now **hears Pause and Cancel** between items, and its progress counts up as it goes. It used to sit at zero and finish whatever you pressed.
- A move that **gathers items from several folders into one** asks first, with how many and where they all go. Search results dropped on the search's own folder used to land there without a word.
**Undo**
- **Ctrl+Z brings back what you deleted**: the batch you sent to the Recycle Bin, from where it was. Deletes from Find duplicates too.
- A copy or move that was **cancelled or stopped halfway can be undone**: what it had done goes back, and the folders it made go once empty. Files that replaced others stay, since the ones they replaced are gone.
- Each transfer's card has an **Undo** button when there's something to take back.
- Undo **survives a restart**, and an undo that couldn't finish (a file in use, say) keeps the rest: Ctrl+Z tries again.
**Big selections**
- Dragging 20,000 search results reads them once per drag instead of on every move of the mouse.
- The **Drop bin** takes thousands of items without slowing down: it checks for doubles at once and only draws the rows in sight.
**Also**
- Settings → Files: **Show items for our other apps that aren't installed**. Turn it off and Scan with SpaceScan, Add to Shelf, Add to Reel and Back up with Backup Labs appear only once that app is on the PC.

## 1.0.63 | 2026-10-29 | Bigger searches, faster deletes, your own sizes and dates
Three wishes from a user's mail.
**Search**
- A search in subfolders no longer just stops at 10,000 results. It **pauses** there, and **Enter carries on** from where it stopped, as many times as you like.
- Pick where it pauses in Settings → Files: 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 or **No limit**.
**Custom size and dates**
- The funnel button beside the filter box has **Custom size…**: at least, at most, or both, each in B, KB, MB or GB.
- And **Custom dates…**: modified on or after a date, before a date, or between the two.
- Both write their terms into the filter box (size:1.5MB-2GB, after:2026-10-01 before:2026-10-15), so they narrow the folder as you type and work in a search through subfolders too.
**Delete**
- **Delete sends the whole selection to the Recycle Bin in one go**: one progress window and, if you have Windows ask, one question, instead of one per file. 2,000 small files took about 9 seconds instead of a minute.

## 1.0.62 | 2026-10-28 | Focus where you'd expect it, and a portable copy
A long-time tester's newest notes: everything File Labs should keep track of for you, it now does.
**Keeping your place**
- **Open path** in Everything or Open-Shell, and **Show in folder** in a browser, now leave the keyboard on the file in File Labs. The hidden Explorer window File Labs takes over could keep the focus and hand it back to the other app when it closed.
- What you paste or drop is **selected where it lands**, the first of it in sight, so three new files among a hundred are easy to find.
- **Each tab keeps its selection and how far it was scrolled** while you're in another one.
**The selection rectangle**
- Holding it past the top or bottom of the list now scrolls smoothly to the end. Before, the list kept jumping back to where you started, and the count went 50, 100, 50, 100.
- It no longer runs over the column headers.
**Transfers and archives**
- The Transfers window is **as tall as its cards**: one copy, one card, and it grows as more start. The speed and its chart show only while something runs.
- **Drag files out of a ZIP or TAR** to the other pane, or to any app, and Ctrl+C copies them too. They are copied, and the archive keeps them.
- A ZIP made with tar, whose paths start with "./", now shows its files instead of a folder called ".", and a name that starts with a dot keeps it.
**Also**
- **Drag a folder, a shortcut to one, or a tab onto Quick access** to pin it there.
- Text size in Settings now applies to the Settings window too.
- What's new goes to the taskbar when minimized, like Settings.
- No row tooltip over the empty space to the right of the columns in Details.
**Portable**
- A **portable zip** comes with every release: unpack it anywhere, even on a stick. It keeps its settings in a Data folder beside it, installs nothing, and leaves the installed File Labs and its links with the other apps alone.

## 1.0.61 | 2026-10-27 | Small files, several at once
**Faster copies**
- Copying many small files is **about twice as fast** on an SSD: files under 16 MB now go four at a time. 1,200 small files on this PC's NVMe drive took 0.31–0.38 s instead of 0.56–0.62 s.
- It happens only where it helps: when both drives are SSDs or network shares. To or from a spinning disk, files still go one by one, so the heads don't jump between them, and big files always go one by one.
- It's on in Settings → Transfers, **Copy small files several at once**, if you ever want it off.

## 1.0.60 | 2026-10-27 | Open archives like folders, mount disk images
**Archives**
- **Enter on a ZIP, TAR or TAR.GZ opens it like a folder**, right in the pane: go into its folders, see sizes and dates, and the path shows where you are inside it.
- Enter on a file inside opens a copy of it. **Ctrl+F5** extracts what you selected to the other pane, or right-click → Extract to… anywhere; files already there are left as they were.
- In a ZIP you can also **add**: drop or paste files into it. **Del** takes items out, after asking, since the Recycle Bin can't hold them.
- Extract here and Extract to folder now work for TAR and TAR.GZ too.
**Disk images**
- Right-click an ISO, IMG, VHD or VHDX → **Mount**: Windows mounts it and it opens in a new tab as a drive. Eject is in that drive's right-click menu.

## 1.0.59 | 2026-10-27 | Duplicates across folders, and by name
**Find duplicates**
- Look in **several folders at once**: select two or more folders and right-click → Find duplicates in these folders, or right-click a folder's empty space → Find duplicates here and in the other pane. In the window, the folder names at the top open a list to add another folder or take one out.
- Choose what counts as a duplicate: **Same content** (the bytes, as before), **Same name and size**, or **Same name**. The last two read nothing, so they're instant even on a big drive, and they find look-alikes rather than proven copies; Delete says so before anything goes.
- A file inside two of the folders you picked is counted once.

## 1.0.58 | 2026-10-27 | Search by size, date, pattern and text inside files
**Search**
- The **funnel button** beside the filter box sets what else to look for: a size, when it was changed, a regular expression on the name, or **text inside files**. Each choice is written into the box, so you can see it and edit it.
- Or type the terms yourself, beside the words of the name: size:>100MB, size:1MB-100MB, within:7d, after:2026-10-01, re:"^IMG_\d+", content:"invoice".
- Typing filters the folder as before, now with sizes and dates too. Enter searches every subfolder, and that's where text inside files is read (files up to 64 MB; ones kept only in the cloud are skipped, not downloaded).
- A term that can't be read, like a broken regular expression, is said at the bottom instead of quietly finding nothing.
